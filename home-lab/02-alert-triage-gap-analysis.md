## What I did
Manually investigated the alert generated by the brute-force self-attack,
examining the raw event details (timestamp, rule description, severity,
source) instead of just confirming an alert existed.

## What I learned
The alert showed rule level 5 ("PAM: User login failed") for only ONE
event, despite Hydra making 6 login attempts and ultimately succeeding
with the correct password. There was no higher-severity correlation alert
for the repeated rapid attempts, and no alert at all for the successful
login - the most critical part of the attack.

## What I found
This is a real detection gap: a single low-severity failed-login alert
does not, on its own, indicate the attack failed or was handled. The
absence of a "brute force detected" correlation rule and the missing
successful-login alert mean this attack would likely have gone unnoticed
by an analyst who only checked severity level and moved on.

## Why this matters
Real alert triage isn't just "read the severity number." A low severity
alert can hide a successful attack if detection rules aren't tuned to
catch patterns (multiple failures in a short window) and critical events
(a success following failures). This is exactly the kind of judgment call
a Tier 1 analyst needs to make - and exactly the kind of gap a more mature
SOC would close by writing a custom correlation rule, which is a natural
next step for this lab.
